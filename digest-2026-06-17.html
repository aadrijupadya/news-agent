A few that stand out to us:

  1. Petrobras (PBR) tops the list at an AI Score of 81. The Brazilian oil major is famous for a massive (if variable) payout. The catch: it's tied to crude, and with oil sliding below $80 today, that's a near-term headwind. AltIndex has it down about 10% over the past three months. High income, higher risk.

  2. Energy Transfer (ET) and Enterprise Products (EPD) are the steady ones. These are pipeline operators, the toll roads of energy, and they tend to throw off reliable income that's far less sensitive to the daily price of oil than a driller like Petrobras.

  3. Capital Southwest (CSWC) is a business development company, a structure built to pass income straight through to shareholders, and one of the few names here actually up over the past three months (about +10%).

One note: AltIndex flags that it's been a rough stretch for dividend stocks lately (Concentrix is down roughly 16% over three months). "Boring" doesn't mean risk-free. But while one company gets a $3 trillion valuation for losing money, these are businesses getting paid to do what they do, and paying you to wait.

📰 Market Headlines

Stocks were mixed as Wall Street braced for the Fed. The Dow rose about 1% while the S&P 500 and Nasdaq slipped, with money rotating out of high-flying tech ahead of tomorrow's decision. The FOMC began its two-day meeting today, with the rate call landing Wednesday at 2 p.m. ET (May retail sales drop the same morning at 8:30). It's Kevin Warsh's first meeting as chair, and after last week's hot CPI and PPI, the Fed is widely expected to hold at 3.50% to 3.75%.

Oil dropped below $80 for the first time since March. Crude fell about 4% as the US and Iran signed a memorandum of understanding, brokered by Pakistan, reopening the Strait of Hormuz and clearing Iran to sell oil again. Talks on the nuclear issue and sanctions run for 60 days, with the formal deal due to be signed Friday in Switzerland. It isn't airtight, though: Iran has already warned Israel to halt its strikes in southern Lebanon, so the truce could still wobble.

SpaceX is buying Cursor for $60 billion. The all-stock deal pushes Musk's empire straight into the AI coding race, and it's a big reason the stock popped again today.

SpaceX briefly became the fourth-largest company on earth. After Friday's record IPO, SPCX has climbed more than 45%, touching a market cap near $2.96 trillion and passing both Amazon and Microsoft. The mania spilled into the new leveraged SpaceX ETFs too, which reportedly traded around $3 billion today, record second-day volume for an ETF. We dug into what it all means for the income investor up top.

Palantir got a fresh stamp of approval. Wolfe Research resumed coverage of Palantir (PLTR), calling it "too big to ignore" on the back of 85% year-over-year revenue growth and a $385 billion addressable market. It's still one of the most divisive names in tech, loved by retail, doubted by the valuation hawks.

Two more tech names announced layoffs. Robinhood (HOOD) said it will cut about 10% of staff (roughly 290 jobs) and slipped around 1.5%, while Rackspace (RXT) is trimming about 15% of its global workforce to save $75 to $85 million a year. Even in an AI boom, the efficiency drive rolls on.

TSMC looks cheap to Goldman. Goldman Sachs called Taiwan Semiconductor (TSM) undervalued versus its fundamentals after dozens of investor meetings, with custom AI chips the number-one investor theme and pricing power pointing to more profit upside. It's the quiet backbone of the entire AI trade.
