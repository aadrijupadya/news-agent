📬 Gold Pays You Nothing. Here's How Some Investors Get Paid Anyway.

Gold has had a rough few weeks. The metal sits around $4,140 per ounce as of June 23, down about 1.3% on the day, off roughly 9% over the past month, and sitting about 26% below its January 2026 record near $5,600. The driver is a sharp repricing of Fed expectations: markets are now pricing nearly a 90% chance of at least one rate hike by year-end, up from 57% just a week earlier, with the 10-year Treasury yield around 4.50% and the dollar firming.

Both BofA and Deutsche Bank have revised their outlooks to include a 2026 hike. Higher yields raise the opportunity cost of holding an asset that pays nothing, and gold pays nothing.

That zero-yield problem is the whole tension here. Physical gold and GLD, the big gold ETF, carry a 0.00% dividend yield. GLD actually charges a 0.40% expense ratio, so holding it costs you money rather than paying you.

The optimists point to structural demand: central banks bought gold at a strong first-quarter pace in Q1 2026, and a record 45% say they plan to add more, per the World Gold Council. Gold is still up about 24% over the past year. The pessimists note that rising-rate environments are exactly when zero-yield assets face the most pressure.

The income workaround is royalty and streaming companies.

Franco-Nevada (FNV) and Wheaton Precious Metals (WPM) finance miners in exchange for royalties or fixed-price metal streams, carrying no mining operating costs. Wheaton posted record Q1 2026 revenue of $901.5 million, up 91.6% year over year, with net earnings up 129%. Franco-Nevada ran gross margins above 80%. Both raise dividends consistently: Royal Gold (RGLD) has 25 straight years of increases.

The caveat is that these yields are small, ranging from roughly 0.6% to 0.9%. The pitch is durable, fast-growing dividends plus gold-price leverage, not a fat current paycheck.

Senior miners offer more current income but more risk. Newmont (NEM) posted an all-time record quarterly free cash flow of $3.1 billion in Q1 2026 and yields around 1.0%.

Barrick Mining (ticker B) carries the highest current yield of this group at around 2.3%.

The bear case is straightforward: miner earnings are highly leveraged to the gold price, so if gold keeps pulling back, cash flows and dividends compress fast.

The Bottom Line:

These are stocks with equity risk, not gold bars with a coupon attached. But they can be a great source of gold exposure with a side of dividends!

📰 Market Headlines

Monday's tape split cleanly down the middle: the Dow gained while tech got hit hard. The Dow closed at 51,712.53, up 0.29%, while the S&P 500 fell 0.37% to 7,472.98 and the Nasdaq dropped 1.32% to 26,166.60. Rising Treasury yields pressured high-valuation tech names, and a pair of ugly individual stories in AI land did the rest of the damage.

Alphabet took a gut punch from two high-profile defections. GOOGL fell more than 5% after Noam Shazeer, a key architect of Google's Gemini models, left for OpenAI, and Nobel Prize-winning DeepMind VP John Jumper departed for Anthropic. The exits raise pointed questions about Alphabet's ability to hold its top AI talent as rivals accelerate toward their own IPOs.

Caterpillar crossed $1,000 for the first time and kept going. CAT hit an all-time high of $1,021.25, up 3.6%, after Chevron and Microsoft announced Project Kilby, a 20-year, 2.67-gigawatt power deal for a West Texas AI data center. Caterpillar's Solar Turbines subsidiary supplies capacity for the project, and CAT alone accounted for roughly 180 points of the Dow's gain on the day.

Micron jumped 6.82% after landing Anthropic as a customer. MU jumped after unveiling a memory-supply deal with the AI startup, a signal that high-bandwidth memory is no longer a commodity but a critical piece of AI hardware that major labs are now locking up directly with chipmakers.

Super Micro surged on a new Nvidia platform launch, while Palantir told a very different story. SMCI jumped 15.66% to $35.46 after unveiling a data center rack solution built on Nvidia's Vera Rubin NVL4 platform, with volume running about 142% above its three-month average. PLTR, by contrast, hit a 52-week low of $119.76, down more than 6% on the day and now sitting 32% lower in 2026 as software stocks broadly struggled.

🏆 AltIndex Stock of the Day: Agnico Eagle Mines (AEM)

Agnico Eagle Mines is one of the world's largest gold producers, with mines and projects across Canada, Finland, and Mexico. It sits at the low-cost end of the senior-producer spectrum, which means when gold prices run, the profits run faster.

PlanBench-XL: Evaluating Long-Horizon Planning of LLM Tool-Use Agents in Large-Scale Tool Ecosystems
This paper introduces a benchmark for evaluating how well LLM agents can plan and use tools across thousands of available options in realistic, long-horizon tasks. It matters because current agent evaluations use small tool sets, but real-world applications require navigating massive tool ecosystems like enterprise APIs, making this a critical gap in understanding agent capabilities.
OpenRath: Session-Centered Runtime State for Agent Systems
OpenRath proposes a session-centered architecture for managing runtime state in multi-step agent systems, providing a standardized way to track and manipulate agent execution context. This is significant because agent systems currently lack consensus on state management, leading to brittleness and difficulty debugging multi-turn interactions—a foundational infrastructure problem.
EnterpriseClawBench: Benchmarking Agents from Real Workplace Sessions
This benchmark evaluates AI agents using traces and tasks extracted from actual enterprise workplace sessions rather than synthetic data. It matters because it grounds agent evaluation in real-world complexity, revealing gaps between lab performance and what actually works in production environments with genuine user workflows.
Grouped Query Experts: Mixture-of-Experts on GQA Self-Attention
The paper combines Grouped Query Attention (GQA) with Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) to create more efficient large language models that maintain performance while reducing memory and compute costs. This is novel because it addresses a practical bottleneck in scaling LLMs by making the computationally expensive attention mechanism more efficient while preserving model quality.
World Action Models: A Survey
This survey comprehensively reviews world action models—systems that predict how agents' actions affect their environment, a core component of planning and RL. It matters because understanding the landscape of action modeling approaches is essential for building embodied AI systems and agents that reason about consequences before acting.
