The Last Real Edge Retail Has Over Wall Street

Let's be honest about investing as a regular person. Wall Street beats you on almost everything. They have faster execution, armies of analysts, direct lines to management, and computers sitting in the same building as the exchange. On speed and access, you cannot win.

But there's one area where we think the little guy can actually get an edge: alternative data.

Alternative data is all the information about a company that shows up before it hits the financial statements. Web traffic. App downloads. Hiring sprees. Social media sentiment. Insider buying. It's the stuff that tells you a business is heating up while the stock is still cheap. And for the first time, retail investors can access it through tools built for them. We think it's the last real bastion of edge regular people have over the institutions.

Our partners at AltIndex score thousands of stocks on a 0-100 scale using exactly this kind of data. We pulled some of their flagged picks from the past two years to show you what "finding the home runs" can actually look like:

Note: these are some of the best outcomes, not the average ones. Not every stock AltIndex flags goes up, plenty go nowhere or fall, and returns like 1,460% are extraordinary, not normal. 📰 Market Headlines

Dow futures jumped about 250 points to kick off June, with Nvidia leading the way. The chip giant unveiled its first Arm-based PC chip at Computex, called the N1X, which will power a new line of RTX Spark Windows laptops from Microsoft, Dell, HP, Asus, Lenovo, and MSI this fall. It's Nvidia's first real move into the PC market, which has long belonged to Intel, AMD, Qualcomm, and Apple. The stock keeps finding new rooms in the house to take over.

The dumbest trade of the week: Virgin Galactic (SPCE) is up around 165% over the past month, and a big chunk of it appears to be Redditors confusing the ticker with SpaceX's upcoming IPO (which will trade as SPCX). One is a struggling space-tourism company burning cash. The other is Elon's rocket empire. They are not the same company. That's roughly how careful the market feels right now.

Michael Burry called the Nvidia-Elon Musk AI deal "Fugazi," his word for fake. Burry published a detailed breakdown alleging that Nvidia sold $5.4 billion in chips to a shell company called Valor, booked it as revenue, then invested $1.9 billion of its own money back into that same entity, which now holds the chips xAI uses to run Grok. His argument is that the structure was built to move credit risk off the balance sheet. Every piece is legal and disclosed. We've said before that we think Burry is wrong about the broader AI bubble, but this specific accounting question is worth understanding rather than ignoring.

Masayoshi Son says the AI revolution is "50x bigger" than the dot-com boom. The SoftBank founder told CNBC in Paris that "this is just like the beginning of the internet," a day after committing 75 billion euros (about $87 billion) to build AI data centers in France. Son has been early on enough megatrends that his enthusiasm is worth noting, even if 50x is the kind of number that's impossible to prove until it happens.

Berkshire Hathaway is buying homebuilder Taylor Morrison for $6.8 billion in cash. The deal pays $72.50 per share, a 24% premium, and marks one of the first major moves under new CEO Greg Abel. Buffett praised the work, saying "Greg did that faster than I could have done it." With Berkshire sitting on a cash hoard near $400 billion, this looks like a signal that Abel is ready to put some of it to work in beaten-down housing.

The $25,000 day trading rule dies on June 4. The SEC and FINRA are eliminating the Pattern Day Trader rule that's required a $25,000 minimum balance to day trade actively since 2001. Brokers will stop counting your day trades and stop the 90-day restrictions. More access for small accounts is genuinely good news, but on the other hand, active day trading is one of the most reliable ways for retail investors to lose money. Just because you can now doesn't mean you should!

The next generation of AI chips is splitting into two strategies. CoreWeave just became one of the first to deploy Nvidia's new Vera Rubin NVL72 system, the high-end, liquid-cooled, expensive-memory approach. Meanwhile Intel just unveiled its Crescent Island GPU, which deliberately skips pricey High Bandwidth Memory (HBM) memory for cheaper LPDDR5X and runs in air-cooled servers. Put simply: Nvidia’s is built for raw power, and Intel’s is built for cheap, efficient inference. Both bets exist because nobody knows exactly which one AI workloads will need in three years’ time… that’s how quickly the tech is developing and changing!

MicroStrategy sold bitcoin for the first time ever. The company offloaded 32 bitcoin for roughly $2.5 million, a tiny sliver of its 843,000-coin treasury, but a symbolic crack in Michael Saylor's years-long "never sell" mantra. When the most committed bitcoin bull on the planet starts trimming, even a little, we’re definitely paying attention. It’s not exactly a vote of confidence!

This paper presents a system for automatically generating reusable AI skills by distilling knowledge from expert models, enabling smaller models to acquire specialized capabilities. Trust-Region Behavior Blending for On-Policy Distillation
The paper introduces a new method for distilling knowledge from teacher to student policies in reinforcement learning while staying close to the original policy distribution using trust-region constraints. This matters because it solves a key problem in policy distillation: preventing the student from diverging too far from the original behavior while still improving performance.
LongTraceRL: Learning Long-Context Reasoning from Search Agent Trajectories with Rubric Rewards
This work trains language models to perform long-horizon reasoning by learning from the trajectories of search agents, using rubric-based rewards to guide learning. It's novel because it combines behavioral cloning from agent trajectories with structured reward signals to improve models' ability to reason over extended contexts.
Representation Forcing for Bottleneck-Free Unified Multimodal Models
The paper proposes a technique to eliminate information bottlenecks in unified multimodal models that process both vision and language by constraining intermediate representations. This is important because multimodal models often suffer from capacity constraints at fusion points, and this approach directly addresses that architectural limitation.
GrepSeek: Training Search Agents for Direct Corpus Interaction
