📊 Warsh Just Slammed the Door on Rate Cut Hopes

Fed Chair Kevin Warsh doubled down on the central bank's hawkish stance on Wednesday, making it crystal clear: if you thought the Fed would tolerate inflation running above its 2% target, you're going to be disappointed. Speaking at the ECB forum in Portugal, Warsh reaffirmed the Fed's commitment to price stability and, when pressed about President Trump's desire for lower rates, insisted the Fed is "going to be an independent central bank" with "no changes" to that independence.

The real numbers backing his hawkishness: the Fed's preferred inflation gauge (core PCE) hit 3.4% in May, the highest since October 2023. Fed officials just revised inflation projections upward too, now seeing headline inflation at 3.6% and core at 3.3%, both significantly higher than their previous forecasts of 2.7%. Warsh also emphasized he wants a smaller Fed balance sheet and prefers interest rates as the primary policy tool, suggesting no pivot toward quantitative easing or other experimental measures.

But the thing is, Warsh dodged every question about rate timing and AI's inflation impact, saying he's "meeting in four weeks" and doesn't want to give forward guidance. Translation: the Fed is either still in debate mode or Warsh is going to remain tight-lipped outside of FOMC meetings.

The takeaway overall though is that Warsh is signaling a hawkish, inflation-focused Fed that won't be pushed around by politics. We’ll see, though, if dropping oil prices change things for inflation (and for rate cut possibilities!).

New Rule Targets $1.1 Billion in Drug Markups

The Trump administration proposed a rule Thursday to stop hospitals from

marking up discounted drugs for Medicare patients, projecting $1.1 billion in savings for consumers next year.

The current 340B program lets hospitals serving low-income patients buy drugs at steep discounts, then bill insurers far more. One prostate cancer drug costs hospitals about $700 per dose but brings in roughly $5,000.

The program now spans

more than 40% of U.S. hospitals. The average senior on Medicare Part B who receives one of these drugs would save about $800 a year in co-payments.

Trump's first administration attempted a similar cut, but the Supreme Court

struck it down

in 2022. The new rule would take effect in January.

Vatican Excommunicates 6 Rebel Bishops

The Vatican excommunicated six bishops of the ultraconservative Society of Saint Pius X on Thursday after the group consecrated four new bishops, one American, without Pope Leo XIV's approval.

The breakaway society rejected 1960s reforms that swapped Latin Mass for local languages and opened the church to other faiths. Some 15,000 supporters watched the five-hour unsanctioned consecration ceremony, complete with $92.50 commemorative wine boxes .

Pope Leo, the first American pope, had

begged the group to "please turn back," calling the plan a "sin of extreme gravity."

The Vatican also invalidated confessions and marriages performed by the society's roughly 700 priests and warned that formal supporters face excommunication too. Its American branch, headquartered in Kansas, claims 25,000 adherents.

Engineering & Research
Residual Context Diffusion Language Models

State-of-the-art block-wise Diffusion Large Language Models (dLLMs) rely on a remasking mechanism that decodes only the most confident tokens and discards the rest. Recycling computation from the discarded tokens is beneficial, as these tokens retain contextual information useful for subsequent decoding iterations. Residual Context Diffusion is a module that converts these discarded token representations into contextual residuals and injects them back for the next denoising step. It consistently improves frontier dLLMs in terms of accuracy with minimal extra computation overhead across a wide range of benchmarks.

Introducing Devin Security Swarm

Devin Security Swarm is a cost-effective and accurate way to find security vulnerabilities in complex codebases. It uses a new architecture for whole-codebase reasoning called Agentic MapReduce. Devin maps relevant signals across a repository, fans out focused agents over bounded shards, reduces their findings to one report, then verifies serious vulnerabilities in isolated sandboxes before marking them confirmed. Cognition has published extensive documentation and technical materials about Agentic MapReduce.

Cutting Edge AI Research
PerceptionRubrics: Calibrating Multimodal Evaluation to Human Perception
This paper develops evaluation metrics for multimodal AI systems that are calibrated to match human perceptual judgments rather than relying on traditional proxy metrics. It's significant because most multimodal evaluations (vision-language models, image generation, etc.) currently rely on imperfect automated metrics that don't correlate well with what humans actually perceive as good quality.
TurboServe: Serving Streaming Video Generation Efficiently and Economically
TurboServe optimizes the serving infrastructure for video generation models, reducing latency and computational costs through novel batching and scheduling strategies tailored to streaming video workloads. This matters because video generation is computationally expensive and currently impractical for real-time applications, so infrastructure improvements directly enable new use cases.
Domain Arithmetic: One-Shot VLA Adaptation under Environmental Shifts
This paper enables Vision-Language-Action (VLA) robots to adapt to new environments with only one demonstration by learning composable domain transformations rather than retraining from scratch. It's novel because it applies arithmetic operations on learned representations to handle environmental shifts, achieving generalization typically requiring multiple examples or fine-tuning.
CausalMix: Data Mixture as Causal Inference for Language Model Training
CausalMix frames optimal data mixture selection for LLM training as a causal inference problem, enabling more principled decisions about which data to include during training. This addresses a fundamental problem in LLM training—that the quality and relevance of training data significantly impacts performance—with a theoretically grounded approach rather than heuristics.
MemSyco-Bench: Benchmarking Sycophancy in Agent Memory
This benchmark measures whether AI agents exhibit sycophancy (telling users what they want to hear) when given persistent memory across interactions. It's important because as agents gain memory and multi-turn capabilities, understanding their tendency to become overly agreeable is critical for trustworthiness and real-world deployment.
