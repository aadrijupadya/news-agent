Hello.

While the world was glued to war headlines, Big Tech quietly made the largest spending commitment in corporate history.

We're not talking about a few billion here and there. We're talking about $650 to $720 BILLION dollars in AI infrastructure spending in 2026 alone. That's more than the GDP of Switzerland. In a single year. On servers and chips.

And this week, the bets got even bigger. 

  • Meta just signed a $21 billion deal with CoreWeave for AI cloud capacity.

  • Intel locked in a multi-generation AI chip deal with Google.

  • Amazon is on track to spend $200 billion this year. Google, $175 billion. Meta, up to $135 billion. Microsoft, $120 billion.

But every investor is still asking the same question right now: Is this the greatest infrastructure buildout since the railroads... or is this the dot-com bubble wearing a new outfit?

Let's break it down.

This is not financial advice. Always do your own research. Past performance doesn’t guarantee future results.

🤖 The $700 Billion AI Bet

Big Tech's combined AI capital expenditure for 2026 is projected at $650 to $720 billion. That's a 70% increase from 2025 levels. And roughly 75% of it (around $450 to $500 billion) is going directly to AI chips, servers, and data centers.

Goldman Sachs estimates that AI infrastructure spending will drive about 40% of S&P 500 earnings growth this year. Nearly half of the stock market's profit growth is being powered by companies building AI infrastructure.

This week alone brought two massive announcements:

  • Meta expanded its deal with CoreWeave to $21 billion (up from $14.2 billion previously), making the total partnership worth $35 billion through 2032. Meta is essentially renting massive AI computing power rather than building it all in-house.

  • Intel and Google deepened their AI chip partnership, a multi-generation deal focused on custom processors designed to handle AI workloads more efficiently. The signal here: as AI moves from training models to actually running them, CPUs matter just as much as GPUs.

Here's the full breakdown of who's spending what this year:

  • Amazon: $200 billion (the biggest spender by far)

  • Google/Alphabet: $175 to $185 billion

  • Meta: $115 to $135 billion

  • Microsoft: $120+ billion

  • Oracle: $50 billion

Eighteen months ago, the combined total was around $380 billion. It has nearly doubled since then.

Who's Getting Rich Off This AI Buildout

Once again, we present to you: the picks and shovels companies that are crushing it.

Because during a gold rush, you don't need to find gold. You just need to sell shovels.

  • Nvidia remains the undisputed king. Revenue for fiscal year 2026 is hitting $215.9 billion, up 65% from the previous year.

  • TSMC is posting roughly 30% revenue growth this year, manufacturing the advanced chips that power AI systems.

  • CoreWeave, which went public in March 2025, is already up about 85% since its IPO and jumped another 4% today on the Meta deal. Nvidia owns an estimated 9% of the company.

  • Marvell is riding the custom AI chip wave. Barclays recently upgraded the stock to Overweight with a $150 price target, and data center revenue is growing nearly 37% year over year.

The Catch

All of this assumes AI will generate enormous revenue. But the math is still shaky.

OpenAI and Anthropic, the two biggest pure-play AI companies, are doing a combined $55 billion in annualized revenue. Impressive, but a fraction of the $700 billion being poured into infrastructure this year. 95% of organizations are getting zero measurable return on their generative AI investments. And Morgan Stanley has warned the buildout "has become so large that it no longer supports paying any price", comparing it to dot-com era telecom spending.

Bull case: AI is real, the productivity gains are coming, and the picks-and-shovels companies get paid regardless of which AI models win.

Bear case: $700 billion a year is a canyon-sized gap from $54 billion in revenue, and someone will be left holding a lot of expensive servers with nobody to rent them.

Either way, $700 billion is the biggest corporate bet in history. And 2026 is the year we start finding out if it pays off.

Israel Agrees to Lebanon Talks Under US Pressure

Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu

authorized direct negotiations with Lebanon on Thursday, following U.S. pressure to scale back deadly Israeli strikes in Beirut the previous day.

The move came after calls with President Trump and White House envoy Steve Witkoff, who reportedly urged Netanyahu to reduce the Lebanon offensive to protect the ceasefire between the U.S. and Iran.

Talks are expected to begin next week

at the State Department in Washington.

Netanyahu maintained that the two-week U.S.-Iran ceasefire

does not cover Lebanon . Iran and Hezbollah have warned of retaliation if Israeli strikes there continue.

The negotiations aim to disarm Hezbollah and potentially establish peaceful relations between Israel and Lebanon, two countries technically at war since 1948.

DOJ Opens Antitrust Probe of NFL Media Deals

The Department of Justice opened an

investigation into the NFL over whether the league is using anticompetitive business tactics. Game broadcasts have been scattered across streaming services and networks, forcing fans to spend upwards of $1,000 a year to watch every game.

Under fire is the

Sports Broadcasting Act of 1961 , which granted the NFL special antitrust protections to negotiate league-wide TV deals. Senator Mike Lee (R-UT) has already requested a formal review of the league's exemption status.

FCC Chairman Brendan Carr went even further, saying the league may be pushing so many games behind paywalls that its antitrust protections are no longer justified .

The NFL defended its model, stating that over 87% of its games air on free broadcast television and that games are always available free in competing teams' local markets.

Airlines Hike Fees to Check Luggage

Delta Air Lines and Southwest Airlines joined United and JetBlue this week in

raising checked bag fees by $10, as carriers seek to offset soaring jet fuel costs linked to escalating tensions in the Middle East.

First checked bags will

now cost $45 and second bags $55 on most routes. Delta is also raising third-bag fees by $50, bringing that total to $200.

Jet fuel, which averaged about $85 to $90 a barrel in February, has surged since then, driven by disruptions around the Strait of Hormuz, a vital chokepoint for global oil shipments.

Analysts warn that the fee increases are likely permanent. One expert noted that once baggage fees go up, they tend to stay there .

Launching S3 Files, making S3 buckets accessible as file systems

Amazon S3 Files is a new capability that allows any S3 bucket to be mounted and accessed as a fully-featured file system directly from EC2 instances, containers, or Lambda functions, with ~1ms latencies for active data and automatic bidirectional synchronization between the file system and S3 bucket. The service is now available in all commercial AWS Regions and eliminates the traditional tradeoff between S3's object storage benefits and the interactive capabilities of file systems, making it particularly useful for collaborative workloads like AI agents and ML training pipelines that need shared, low-latency access to S3 data.

Introducing Bun as a Runtime for Pulumi

Pulumi now supports Bun as a fully supported runtime (not just a package manager) as of version 3.227.0, allowing developers to execute entire Pulumi programs by setting `runtime: bun` in their Pulumi.yaml with no Node.js required. The integration offers faster package installation and native TypeScript support, though it currently doesn't support policy packs, function serialization, or multi-language components that some projects may still need Node.js for.

The Next Era of the Open Lakehouse: Apache Iceberg™ v3 in Public Preview on Databricks

Databricks launched public preview support for Iceberg v3, which introduces Row Lineage for tracking data changes, Deletion Vectors for 10x faster updates and deletes, and a VARIANT type for querying semi-structured data without schema enforcement. The update eliminates the performance tradeoff between Delta Lake and Iceberg through Unity Catalog's cross-engine governance, allowing teams to write data once and read it across multiple platforms like Snowflake, BigQuery, and Redshift without replication.

Understanding Traceroute

Traceroute works by sending packets with increasing TTL values so each router drops them in sequence and returns an ICMP “Time Exceeded” message, revealing each hop's IP until the destination responds with a different ICMP message. By repeating this process with multiple probes and measuring response times, it maps the path and latency of network traffic, though gaps occur when routers don't send replies.

Think in Strokes, Not Pixels: Process-Driven Image Generation via Interleaved Reasoning
This paper generates images through a stroke-by-stroke process that interleaves reasoning about composition with actual drawing, mimicking how humans create art rather than predicting all pixels at once. This is novel because it breaks image generation into interpretable sequential steps, potentially enabling better control, editing, and understanding of the generative process compared to standard diffusion models.
RAGEN-2: Reasoning Collapse in Agentic RL
This paper identifies and analyzes a critical failure mode in reinforcement learning agents where their reasoning capability degrades over training, despite improved reward optimization. The finding is significant because it reveals a fundamental trade-off between maximizing rewards and maintaining robust reasoning in autonomous agents, which has major implications for deploying RL systems safely.
MARS: Enabling Autoregressive Models Multi-Token Generation
MARS allows autoregressive language models to generate multiple tokens in parallel during inference, improving speed without retraining or architectural changes. This matters because it directly addresses inference latency—a major bottleneck in practical LLM deployment—while remaining compatible with existing models.
INSPATIO-WORLD: A Real-Time 4D World Simulator via Spatiotemporal Autoregressive Modeling
This work uses spatiotemporal autoregressive models to simulate dynamic 3D environments in real-time, enabling physics-aware video generation that maintains consistency across space and time. It's significant because real-time 4D simulation could unlock new capabilities in robotics simulation, game engines, and embodied AI training without expensive physics engines.
SEVerA: Verified Synthesis of Self-Evolving Agents
This paper proposes methods to formally verify that self-modifying AI agents maintain safety properties as they evolve their own code and behavior. It addresses a critical gap in AI safety: ensuring autonomous agents that rewrite themselves remain trustworthy and aligned with their original objectives.
