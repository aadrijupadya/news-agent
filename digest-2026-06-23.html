The First Half of 2026 Was One of the Wildest Market Stretches in Recent Memory

With markets closed on Friday for Juneteenth, we wanted to do a quick debrief of the biggest narratives from the year so far to remind you just how crazy things have been…

The first half of 2026 packed in a major US military raid, an oil price round-trip, a Fed regime change, the largest IPO in history, and an AI spending war that rewarded some players and punished others. Here is every major narrative, and how the market responded.

The AI buildout: The four biggest AI hyperscalers committed more than $700 billion in combined 2026 AI and data-center spending (up roughly 77% from 2025), and the market punished the boldest spenders. Amazon fell 8% after guiding to about $200 billion in capex, while the memory makers that supply the buildout were the clean winners, with Micron crossing a $1 trillion market cap and closing at an all-time high of $1,133.99 on June 18 after more than tripling year to date. Nuclear-for-AI kept making headlines (Meta backed up to 6.6 gigawatts in January), but most nuclear stocks cooled off: Constellation down about 22%, Oklo off about 15%, NuScale down about 17% year to date through mid-June. GE Vernova bucked the group with a roughly 70% gain.

The US strike on Venezuela: A roughly 30-minute special-ops raid captured Maduro, and the counterintuitive result was that oil barely moved. WTI held near $58, Brent held near $60, because Venezuela accounts for less than 1% of an already oversupplied global market. Stocks celebrated anyway: the Dow closed at a record +594.79 points on January 5, the S&P 500 energy index jumped 2.7%, Halliburton surged 7.8%, and Chevron, the lone US operator in-country, added 5.1%.

The US-China AI cold war: Policy headlines whipsawed semiconductor stocks in both directions across January through May. On January 28, China approved 400,000-plus Nvidia H200 chips for ByteDance, Alibaba, and Tencent, sending the VanEck Semiconductor ETF up 1.9% and SK Hynix up 5% in a single session, and Nvidia received US licenses to resume H200 shipments in March. But when Nvidia reported a record $81.6 billion quarter on May 20, it booked zero China data-center revenue and excluded China entirely from its guidance, sending the stock down 1.8% the next day.

AI as national security: Washington formally treated AI as a defense asset in the first half of the year. The Pentagon signed eight frontier-AI firms to classified-network agreements in May, and President Trump issued an AI national-security memo in June. Palantir was the poster child for the theme, reporting revenue up 85% year over year to $1.63 billion in Q1 with US government revenue up 84% - yet the stock was still down roughly 18% on the year as of that May 4 report, a reminder that a good story and a good entry are two different things.

The Fed regime change: President Trump nominated hawk Kevin Warsh to replace Powell on January 30, and markets initially read it as easing Fed-independence fears. The dollar rose about 0.8% while gold futures dropped 11.4% and silver futures fell 31.4% (silver's worst day since March 1980). Warsh took office May 22, and at his June debut meeting he held rates at 3.50%-3.75% but stripped the cut bias entirely. The dot plot flipped to signal a 2026 hike, the 2-year Treasury yield jumped 16 basis points to 4.216% (the biggest Fed-day move since March 2008), and the S&P 500 fell 1.21%.

Iran: US-Israel strikes on February 28 killed Ayatollah Khamenei, with the Strait of Hormuz disrupted in the aftermath, sending Brent crude rocketing roughly 55% from about $72 to near $120 at its peak (one of the largest moves on record). Defense stocks jumped at the outbreak (Northrop +6%, RTX +4.7%, Lockheed +3.4%), but a 14-point framework agreement on June 17 reopened the Strait and round-tripped most of the move, with Brent falling back to around $80, down roughly 36% from the peak, as S&P futures added about 0.8% on the news.

The SpaceX IPO: On June 12, SpaceX priced 555.6 million shares at $135 each, raising $75 billion in the largest IPO in history and debuting on the Nasdaq under SPCX. The stock closed up 20% on day one at roughly $161, then added another 20% the following Monday to $192.50. The rest of the space sector sold off on capital recycling: Rocket Lab fell about 8%, Intuitive Machines dropped 11%, and AST SpaceMobile fell about 12% or more.

The through-line of the first half of the year is that geopolitical and policy shocks moved markets hard and fast but rarely held. Venezuela and Iran both round-tripped, and the Fed's hawkish turn lifted yields in a hurry. The durable bid stayed with the AI buildout and hard assets, while the biggest IPO ever landed right in the middle of peak AI mania.

The Bottom Line

Income investors heading into the rest of 2026 now have a higher-yield backdrop and a lot of unresolved threads to track.

📰 Market Headlines

A peace deal was not signed this weekend, but a 60-day roadmap to peace was agreed on. Today, oil is choppy and stocks are roughly flat as President Trump warned of fresh strikes if Iran’s attacks on Israel continue.

Wall Street shook off a midweek scare and closed the holiday-shortened week green. The Dow closed at 51,564.70 Thursday, the S&P 500 gained 0.93% on the week and the Nasdaq 2.43%, led by a 6.4% single-day jump in the semiconductor index.

The Iran deal survived a shaky weekend. After both presidents signed the interim framework on June 17, the truce wobbled fast: Tehran briefly threatened to close the Strait of Hormuz again over Israeli strikes in Lebanon, and Vice President Vance flew to Switzerland to keep the talks alive. By early Monday, the two sides had agreed on a roadmap to reach a final deal within 60 days, plus a direct line to avoid incidents in the Strait. The hard parts are still unresolved: Iran's uranium enrichment, its highly enriched stockpile, sanctions relief, and up to $25 billion in frozen assets.

The Pentagon handed a uranium miner a $725 million lifeline. Energy Fuels (UUUU) jumped 11.2% after the Department of Defense issued a conditional $725 million financing commitment for rare-earth processing. The company is currently unprofitable, so the financing is conditional, not cash in hand.

Accenture made a $4.2 billion cybersecurity bet. Accenture (ACN) agreed to buy Dragos, runZero and NetRise for about $4.2 billion to build an AI-era industrial cybersecurity business.

Former Fed Chair Alan Greenspan passed away at the age of 100. He was chair for 19 years under four presidents, from Presidents Reagan to George W. Bush (CNBC). He famously warned of “irrational exuberance” in markets in 1996, though the crash didn’t come till 2001.

Software requires different levels of verification depending on its use case. Casual software has limited distribution and loose quality constraints. Business software needs to work, or organizations suffer losses. Acute software is mission-critical and merits the highest levels of scrutiny. You should use AI for reviewing code especially when the diff is huge

Cutting Edge AI Research
PerceptionDLM: Parallel Region Perception with Multimodal Diffusion Language Models
This paper extends diffusion language models to handle multimodal inputs by enabling parallel perception of different image regions, allowing the model to generate text conditioned on visual information. The approach is novel because it scales visual understanding in diffusion models beyond sequential processing, potentially enabling more efficient and accurate vision-language tasks like visual question answering and image captioning.
Multi-Turn Reflective Masking Elicits Reasoning in Mask Diffusion Models
The paper introduces a technique where mask diffusion models iteratively refine their outputs through multi-turn masking and reflection, enabling them to perform complex reasoning tasks typically associated with autoregressive language models. This is significant because it demonstrates that diffusion models—previously seen as primarily generative—can be adapted to perform step-by-step reasoning, potentially offering a different computational paradigm for reasoning tasks.
SproutRAG: Attention-Guided Tree Search with Progressive Embeddings for Long-Document RAG
This paper proposes an improved retrieval-augmented generation system that uses tree search with attention guidance to efficiently retrieve relevant information from long documents while maintaining progressive embedding updates. It matters because handling long-document retrieval remains a critical bottleneck in RAG systems, and this approach specifically addresses the challenge of finding relevant passages in documents that exceed typical context windows.
GateMem: Benchmarking Memory Governance in Multi-Principal Shared-Memory Agents
The paper introduces a benchmark for evaluating how well multi-agent systems can manage shared memory when multiple agents with different goals operate together, addressing security and isolation concerns. This is important because as AI systems become more collaborative, ensuring that one agent cannot maliciously or accidentally corrupt another agent's memory is a critical safety consideration that has been largely unstudied.
